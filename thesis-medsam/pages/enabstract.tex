Medical image segmentation is one of the core tasks in computer-aided diagnosis. In recent years, segmentation methods based on visual foundation models have achieved remarkable progress, among which MedSAM, as a medical domain adaptation of SAM, has become the most widely adopted foundation model for medical image segmentation. However, MedSAM faces two critical challenges in abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation: first, severe dual-level class imbalance between foreground and background as well as among different organ categories, which impedes the learning of small organs; second, the global self-attention mechanism of the ViT encoder inherently lacks the ability to capture local high-frequency features, resulting in over-smoothed organ boundaries.

To address these issues, this thesis proposes two core innovations supplemented by a key ablation study. At the loss level, a joint balanced loss framework called Balance Loss is designed, which models inter-class and intra-class imbalance through Inter-CBL and Intra-CBL respectively. Combined with Dice loss and a two-stage training strategy, it significantly improves the baseline segmentation performance. At the feature level, a Local-Global Adapter (LG-Adapter) is proposed, which leverages dual-path depthwise separable convolution and dilated convolution for local feature fusion. With only 0.15\% additional parameter overhead, it compensates for the high-frequency feature deficiency of ViT. Additionally, a LoRA ($r=4$) frozen-backbone fine-tuning ablation study reveals the limitations of low-rank parameter-efficient fine-tuning for complex multi-organ segmentation tasks under the current experimental conditions, providing important empirical evidence on the limitations of such strategies for the architectural design.

This thesis conducts systematic same-caliber evaluation through a complete ablation path from baseline to the final scheme, with all experiments performed on the abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation task of the FLARE22 dataset under ground-truth box prompt (GT box prompt) conditions. The final scheme achieves a DSC of 0.9620, HD95 reduction of 56.9\%, and ASD reduction of 57.8\% on a 10-case test set, validating the synergistic effect of Balance Loss and LG-Adapter and providing a feasible optimization pathway for the fine-grained application of large models in the medical domain.
