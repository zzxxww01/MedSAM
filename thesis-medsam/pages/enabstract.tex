Medical image segmentation is a fundamental task in computer-aided diagnosis. MedSAM, a medical adaptation of the Segment Anything Model, provides a unified backbone for prompt-based segmentation. However, when applied to abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation, it faces two key limitations: unbalanced training-signal allocation across foreground and background pixels as well as easy and hard pixels, and insufficient local boundary representation in the ViT encoder. This thesis systematically addresses these issues based on MedSAM.

To address training-signal imbalance, we identify and formalize a dual-layer imbalance structure in box-prompt-driven binary segmentation, and design a joint balanced loss framework called Balance Loss. It reconstructs pixel-level supervision through Inter-CBL (inter-class rebalancing via hard negative mining) and Intra-CBL (intra-class hard-pixel weighting), combined with a two-stage training strategy. To strengthen local boundary representation, we compare several fine-tuning routes and finally retain a lightweight Multi-Scale Local Adapter (MSL-Adapter) inserted at the feature bridge, which supplements local spatial information via dual-path depthwise separable convolutions.

Experiments are conducted on 60 training cases (5410 foreground slices in total) and 15 test cases (1295 foreground slices in total) under GT-box-prompt protocol. Experiments demonstrate that Balance Loss consistently improves baseline performance on difficult organs and boundary metrics, while the local adapter provides directionally stable boundary refinement on this basis. The final model achieves DSC 0.9562 and HD95 3.01 mm, with statistical significance confirmed for Balance Loss improvements over baseline ($p<0.001$). Comparison with existing methods demonstrates that the proposed approach outperforms SAMed and Medical SAM Adapter under the same prompt setting.
