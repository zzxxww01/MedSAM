Medical image segmentation is fundamental to computer-aided diagnosis. MedSAM, a medical adaptation of the Segment Anything Model, provides a unified backbone for prompt-based segmentation. However, when applied to abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation, it faces two key limitations: unbalanced training-signal allocation between foreground/background and easy/hard pixels, and insufficient local boundary representation in the ViT encoder. This thesis systematically addresses these issues based on MedSAM.

To improve training-signal allocation, we identify and formalize a dual-layer imbalance structure in box-prompt-driven binary segmentation, and design a joint balanced loss framework called Balance Loss. It reconstructs pixel-level supervision through Inter-CBL (inter-class rebalancing via hard negative mining) and Intra-CBL (intra-class hard-pixel weighting), combined with a two-stage training strategy. To strengthen local boundary representation, we compare several fine-tuning routes and finally retain a lightweight Multi-Scale Local Adapter (MSL-Adapter) inserted at the feature bridge, which supplements local spatial information via dual-path depthwise separable convolutions.

Experiments are conducted on 60 training cases and 15 test cases under GT-box-prompt protocol. Experimental results demonstrate that Balance Loss consistently improves baseline performance on difficult organs and boundary metrics, while the local adapter provides directionally stable boundary refinement on this basis. The final model achieves DSC 0.9562 and HD95 3.01 mm, with statistical significance confirmed for Balance Loss improvements over baseline ($p<0.001$). Comparison with existing methods demonstrates that the proposed approach outperforms SAMed and Medical SAM Adapter under the same prompt setting.
