Medical image segmentation is one of the core tasks in computer-aided diagnosis. In recent years, segmentation methods based on visual foundation models have achieved remarkable progress, among which MedSAM, as a medical domain adaptation of SAM, has become the most widely adopted foundation model for medical image segmentation. However, MedSAM faces two critical challenges in abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation: first, severe dual-level class imbalance between foreground and background as well as among different organ categories, which impedes the learning of small organs; second, the global self-attention mechanism of the ViT encoder inherently lacks the ability to capture local high-frequency features, resulting in over-smoothed organ boundaries.

To address these issues, this thesis proposes a systematic ``three-pillar'' improvement framework. The first pillar (loss layer): a joint gradient-balanced loss framework called Balance Loss is designed, which models inter-class and intra-class imbalance through Inter-CBL and Intra-CBL respectively. Combined with Dice loss and a two-stage training strategy, it improves the baseline DSC from 0.941 to 0.960, reduces HD95 by 53.4\%, and reduces ASD by 54.2\%. The second pillar (ablation counter-evidence): through a LoRA frozen-backbone fine-tuning experiment (C2), the inferior performance of DSC=0.879 conclusively demonstrates the indispensability of opening the foundation model backbone for spatial manifold reshaping. The third pillar (feature layer): a Local-Global Adapter (LG-Adapter) is proposed, which leverages dual-path depthwise separable convolution and dilated convolution for multi-scale feature fusion. With only 0.15\% additional parameter overhead, it compensates for the high-frequency feature deficiency of ViT, ultimately achieving optimal performance of DSC=0.9620 and HD95=2.91 on a 40-case test set.

This thesis conducts systematic same-caliber evaluation through a complete ablation path (A0$\rightarrow$A3R3$\rightarrow$C2$\rightarrow$C3), with all experiments performed on the abdominal multi-organ CT segmentation task of the FLARE22 dataset. Experimental results demonstrate that the synergy between Balance Loss and LG-Adapter effectively addresses the class imbalance and local feature deficiency problems of MedSAM in medical segmentation, providing a feasible optimization pathway for the fine-grained application of large models in the medical domain.
